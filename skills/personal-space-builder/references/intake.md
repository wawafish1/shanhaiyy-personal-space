# Portfolio intake

Use this as a question bank, not a questionnaire to dump on the user. First inspect supplied files and prior answers, then ask only questions that affect the next decision. Group related questions and accept answers in stages.

## 1. Purpose and audience

- Who should act after viewing the site: recruiters, hiring managers, prospective customers, collaborators, or several audiences?
- What is the desired action: email, phone call, resume download, project visit, or social-profile visit?
- Where will the link appear: resume, recruiting platform, email signature, social bio, or QR code?

## 2. Positioning

- Preferred displayed name and language.
- Current professional identity and the two or three strengths the visitor should remember.
- Target role or direction. Confirm whether it is present experience or a future plan.
- Location constraints, remote work, relocation, and business-travel availability.
- Desired tone: technical, commercial, editorial, understated, energetic, or another direction.

Do not require a narrow target title when the user intentionally wants a broader positioning. A clear value proposition can replace a rigid job-title label.

## 3. Career evidence

- Resume or structured employment history with dates, employers, roles, and education.
- For each important experience: situation, task, action, collaborators, constraints, result, and evidence.
- Which customers, products, machines, locations, metrics, and documents may be named publicly?
- What must be anonymized or omitted?

Convert duty lists into selected evidence without exaggerating impact. If results are qualitative, keep them qualitative.

## 4. Projects and content practice

- Project name, problem, intended user, current status, working link, repository link, and screenshots.
- Which work was independently completed, collaboratively completed, or assisted by AI tools?
- Social account URLs, platform screenshots, and the date of any follower or activity numbers.
- Should an account card open an on-site screenshot first, or leave the site immediately?

Do not imply that follower counts equal revenue or business outcomes unless evidence supports that claim.

## 5. Visual material

- Portrait candidates, work-site photographs, project interfaces, and personal-life photographs.
- The category and intended meaning of each image.
- Permission to crop, retouch, redact, or generate decorative assets.
- Preferred density and carousel behavior: automatic, manual, pause controls, enlargement, captions, and mobile behavior.

Favor a curated sequence. Preserve originals and keep factual photographs distinct from generated decoration.

## 6. Contact and privacy

Confirm each item independently:

- Public email address.
- Public telephone number.
- Whether the telephone number is also a messaging-account identifier.
- Resume download availability and which resume version is public.
- Public social links.
- Any information that must never be committed to a public repository.

Summarize the final public/private boundary back to the user before publication.

## 7. Brand and interaction

- Existing logo, initials, personal mark, palette, references, and visual dislikes.
- Required sections and navigation order.
- Expected desktop/mobile balance and motion preferences.
- Whether links, modals, carousels, downloads, copy actions, and embedded media have an agreed behavior.

## 8. Delivery

- Local preview only, downloadable bundle, public hosting, or all three.
- Domain, DNS provider, server/provider, operating system, SSH user and port, and existing reverse proxy.
- Whether temporary key-based SSH is preferred. Never request that a password or private key be pasted into chat.
- Public or private source repository, GitHub owner, repository name, and licensing preference.

Before implementation, provide a concise decision summary: audience, positioning, sections, assets, interactions, public data, delivery targets, and unresolved blockers.
